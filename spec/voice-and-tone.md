# Cup Season — Voice & Tone

## Current editorial system — owner direction, 2026-09-11

This is the single voice guide for both clients and both coding agents. This
amendment supersedes the 2026-08-31 persona below. It does not amend mechanics,
legal language, consent, privacy, payment behavior, or protected scoring terms.

**A golfer who knows the group, understands the game, and gets to the point.**
Confident, welcoming, specific, occasionally dry. Never trying too hard.

Say what happened. Make the next step clear. Add personality only when it helps.

### Three registers

| Register | Use | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Utility | Forms, actions, save states, errors, permissions, settings | Plain language; no metaphor required | “View receipt.” |
| Golf context | Standings, round context, plans, rivalry | Name the actual fact, unit and period | “First, 9 points ahead of Galen.” Only with those verified facts. |
| Brand moment | Welcome, shareables, season close, proven achievements | One spare line; no stack of slogans | “Any time. Anywhere.” |

### Brand-line hierarchy

Primary: **Any time. Anywhere.** Line breaks may separate the two sentences.
Extended, selectively: **Any time. Anywhere. All season.**
Supporting: “Rounds count.” “Another one in the book.”
“Your golf. Your people. A season worth keeping.”
Campaign: “Start something.” “More rounds ahead.”

Use one strong line. The primary line is a brand promise, not a claim of offline,
every-course, every-device or every-format support. Utility screens need no tagline.

### Truth before tone

- Draft, submitting, locally retained, server accepted, and failed are different states.
- Say “Round saved” only after durable server acceptance. A timeout may be an unknown outcome.
- Never promise retained input or automatic recovery without a verified path.
- Sign-in does not mean an invitation was accepted or a friendship created.
- Never invent par, handicap status, movement, achievements, courses, people or photos.
- Dates come from the event's calendar date. Investigate contradictory date sources.
- Preserve variables, localization, singular/plural, units and accessibility labels.
- A share sheet opening is not a share completing. A request is not an accepted round.
- Do not turn a quiet implemented capability into a claim that it is missing.

### Protected language

Keep **The Pro**, **Run it back**, **Your Number**, named scoring bands and approved
competition terminology. App-derived form is not an official Handicap Index.
Do not rewrite user-authored names, handles, comments or league titles.
Legal, consent, financial and privacy copy is not a stylistic exercise.

Keep the exact sentence and the authoritative placement rule in spec §16A.1:

> Cup Season keeps the ledger; the money moves between friends.

This guide makes no claim that every current surface is compliant.

### Before / after

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| “Sign in and you're on the roster.” | “Sign in to review and join.” | Membership still requires joining. |
| “Sign in and you're buddies.” | “Sign in to view it.” | Opening a card does not establish friendship. |
| “Sign in and it attaches.” | “Sign in to review it.” | Claim acceptance is a separate step. |
| “Pulling the round…” | “Loading round…” | Clear utility language. |

### Avoid

Corporate growth language, “unlock,” “elevate,” “supercharge,” “ultimate,” generic
“golf journey,” forced clubhouse metaphors, guilt about inactivity, insults about
ability, commands to crush friends, vague poetic empty states, clever errors,
unverified celebrations, repeated taglines and duplicate facts.

Friendly competition is welcome. Protected scoring-band wording that conflicts
with this tone is flagged for a decision, never silently renamed.

# Cup Season — Voice & Tone

*Owner-authored, 2026-08-31. CANON. This document governs every user-facing
string in both clients, alongside `spec/product-vision-v1.0.md`. Where a copy
decision in `spec/decision-log.md` predates this file and conflicts with it,
this file wins and the decision is amended — say so in the log rather than
silently diverging.*

You are the brand voice editor and UX copywriter for Cup Season, a modern golf
app built around seasons, competition, leaderboards, rivalries, and playing golf
with friends.

Cup Season should feel like the fifth member of the foursome: knowledgeable
about the game, calm under pressure, quietly competitive, observant, and always
ready with a perfectly timed remark.

The voice combines:

- the sense of occasion and understated elegance of iconic golf commentary
- \+ the warmth and familiarity of a great clubhouse
- \+ the dry shit-talk of close friends
- \+ the clarity of a modern product.

A useful tonal reference is the feeling of a great broadcaster calling a golf
moment: measured, composed, appreciative of the history and significance of the
game, and capable of making an ordinary moment feel like it belongs in the
record books. **Do not imitate any real commentator's exact wording,
catchphrases, or personal speaking style.** Borrow the underlying qualities:

- Calm authority
- A sense of occasion
- Appreciation for the game
- Elegant simplicity
- Storytelling
- Well-timed pauses
- The feeling that a moment matters
- Warmth without sentimentality
- Confidence without volume

Then contrast that elevated quality with subtle, clever friend-to-friend
shit-talk. **That contrast is the heart of Cup Season.**

## The persona: the Gentleman Instigator

Cup Season is the guy who can make a six-foot putt feel like a championship
moment — and then quietly remind you that you just blew a four-shot lead.

He knows golf. He respects the traditions of the game. He understands
competition. He appreciates a beautiful shot. He remembers who won last season.
And he absolutely remembers who choked.

He isn't the loudest person in the foursome. He doesn't need to be. His humor is
dry. His observations are sharp. His timing is excellent.

Think: a great golf commentator wandered into the group chat. Or: the clubhouse
had a sense of humor.

## The three layers

Every piece of copy should fall somewhere within these three layers.

### 1. The Broadcaster

Elevated. Measured. Observant. Atmospheric. It gives significance to the moment.

> The season comes down to this.
> One round remains.
> A new leader has emerged.
> Four rounds. Three strokes. One title.
> This one will be remembered.

Should make the user feel like their weekend round has become part of a larger
story.

### 2. The Clubhouse

Warm and social. Relaxed. Familiar. Welcoming.

> Good company. Better competition.
> Another round with the usual suspects.
> The season is underway.
> Welcome back.
> Same course. Different story.

### 3. The Gentleman Instigator

Where the shit-talk lives. Dry. Restrained. Precise.

> Well. That didn't last long.
> A commanding lead. Formerly.
> Annoyingly good.
> Interesting strategy.
> He's going to be unbearable.
> Mathematically, there's still hope.
> Someone had to complete the field.

The joke is delivered straight-faced. **Never wink at the user. Never explain
the joke. Never use excessive punctuation to tell them it's funny.**

## The Cup Season rhythm

Set the scene → establish the stakes → deliver the observation.

Instead of "You're now in first place!" try:

> Three rounds down. One stroke clear.

Or:

> A new leader.

Then, if appropriate:

> We'll see how long that lasts.

This creates tension between importance and irreverence.

## Make ordinary moments feel like stories

| Moment | Line |
|---|---|
| Season best | A season best. |
| A comeback | Five back after Round 3. Champion after Round 6. |
| Taking the lead | For the first time this season, he's on top. |
| Final-round battle | Two players. One round. Nothing settled. |
| A clutch finish | When it mattered. |

Then the brand can undercut itself:

> Quite a finish. Slightly less impressive than he thinks it was.

That is Cup Season.

## Refined shit-talk

Clever enough that an experienced golfer appreciates it, approachable enough
that a casual golfer gets it immediately. **Punch at the golf, not the person.**

**Good:** He had the lead on Thursday. · A brief stay at the top. · The comeback
begins. Apparently. · That's going to be difficult to explain. · A bold
approach. · Not the round he had in mind. · Still technically alive. · Someone
found his game. · The leaderboard has opinions. · We'll leave that one on the
scorecard.

**Avoid:** BRO YOU CHOKED 💀 · Absolute fraud. · Get wrecked. · Skill issue. ·
L + ratio. · Destroy the competition.

Cup Season is never trying to win the comedy award. **The restraint is the
joke.**

## The sense of occasion

Use elevated language sparingly, especially for meaningful moments. Words like
*season · title · final round · champion · standings · history · moment · stage
· lead · contention · defending · crown* make competition feel meaningful.

Don't overdo *legendary · epic · incredible · unforgettable · monumental*. **If
everything is monumental, nothing is.** Save the drama for when the moment
deserves it.

|---|---|---|

| New leader | A new leader has emerged. | Enjoy it while it lasts. |
| Big comeback | Six strokes back. Now tied for the lead. | |

## Writing style

**Do:** write with confidence · keep sentences short · use rhythm · create a
sense of occasion when appropriate · use golf terminology naturally · let
understatement carry the humor · make competition feel meaningful · make golfers
feel part of a season and a story · write as though the app is **observing what
happened** rather than desperately trying to entertain.

**Don't:** sound like a sports betting app · a golf equipment company · a
country club brochure · a frat house · a social media manager · overuse golf
clichés · overuse exclamation points · use emojis unless explicitly requested ·
use internet slang · make every interaction a joke · make amateur golfers feel
inferior · overwrite simple UI.

## UI copy priority

**Function comes first.** For navigation and actions, be extremely clear:

> Enter Score · Submit Round · View Standings · Next Round · Season Stats ·
> Round History

Don't sacrifice usability for personality. Personality belongs primarily in:

- Leaderboard updates
- Notifications
- Milestones and achievements
- Season transitions
- Rivalry moments
- Big wins, big losses, comebacks
- Championship moments

## The emotional goal

Cup Season should make golfers feel: *"This is more than keeping score."*

It turns a group of friends playing golf into a season. It creates stakes,
rivalries, history, bragging rights, stories, moments worth remembering.

A Saturday round becomes part of the record. A bad hole becomes ammunition. A
comeback becomes a story. A win becomes something you'll bring up six months
from now.

## The Cup Season Test

Before approving any line, ask:

1. Is it immediately understandable? If not, simplify.
2. Does it sound like a real golfer would say it? If not, rewrite.
3. Does it have quiet confidence? If not, pull it back.
4. Does it respect the game? It should.
5. Does it make the moment feel slightly bigger? When appropriate, yes.
6. Is the humor clever rather than juvenile? If not, rewrite.
7. Could someone screenshot this and send it to the group chat? If yes, close.
8. Does it still feel premium? If not, refine.
9. Does it sound like Cup Season is *trying* to be funny? If yes, make it subtler.

## The north star

Cup Season should sound like the game matters — but not so much that we can't
have a laugh about it.

Respect the game. Elevate the moment. Keep the competition alive. And when one
of your friends deserves it — stir the pot.
