Cup Season · design options · 30 August 2026

Turning the phone sideways.

Live scoring is the one screen in Cup Season whose natural shape is wider than it is tall. Drawn at a real phone's landscape proportions, in the app's own charcoal — so what you are looking at is a preview, not a sketch. Decided: rotate into B, with a toggle to A for the whole round — including where the points were won.

844×390 the canvas 0 landscape rules today portrait-locked on iOS 92.6% of cached holes carry a stroke index D78 already keeps the hole ledger

What's true right now

Nothing supports landscape. There is not a single orientation: landscape rule in the client, and iOS pins the app to portrait in two places in project.yml. So this is a green field — but there is one trap worth knowing before choosing.

The two-column layout you already own doesn't fire on a phone. .playgrid becomes two columns at min-width:960px. A phone in landscape is 844–932px wide. So if rotation were simply allowed today, you would get the stacked portrait layout crushed into a 390px-tall window — the worst of both. Any option below has to move that breakpoint.

Two things already exist that make this cheaper than it looks.
The hole ledger. D78 made every game engine keep cells — who took each hole — with a comment saying the engines "already decide who took each hole and then throw it away." Match play returns 'a'/'b'/'h', skins returns the winner or 'c' for a carry. Where the points were won needs no new maths, and no second source of truth.
The stroke index. api_course_holes.handicap is cached for 5,652 of 6,102 holes across 341 tees — 92.6%. The client already loads it into SI_LOADED and sets SIEST=true when it has to guess, so the card can show SI and be honest about when it is estimated.

It also matters that live scoring is hole-by-hole, not a grid: a hole header with arrows, then one row per player with a −/+ stepper. That already suits a tall screen. So the question is not "how do we fit this sideways" — it is what does turning the phone earn you that portrait cannot give.

Why

  • ✓ It is the only option that shows you something portrait cannot. Rotating earns information, which is the only honest reason to rotate.
  • ✓ It is the mental model golfers already have: the card lives sideways in your pocket.
  • ✓ Read-only sidesteps the 390px height problem entirely — no tap targets to protect.
  • ✓ Answers "how is everyone doing" in one glance, which is the question a foursome actually asks.

Cost

  • ! A second scoring surface to build, and one more thing that can disagree with the first.
  • ! Wants the dormant D34 grid brought back, or something close to it.
  • ! Nine-hole rounds need their own column set.

The decision

Rotate into B. Toggle to A. They stop being rivals the moment they share a screen: turning the phone gives you the hole you are standing on plus the side games that were buried below the fold, and one tap on HOLE / CARD opens the whole round — where everybody stands and, when a game is on, which holes paid.

Three things make this cheaper than it first looked, and all three already exist:

  • The layout. B is one media query — .playgrid moving from 960px down to around 740 so it engages on a landscape phone instead of just a laptop.
  • The ledger. D78 already has every engine keep cells — who took each hole — precisely so a view like this never has to re-derive it. Match play hands back 'a'/'b'/'h'; skins hands back the winner or a carry. "Where the points were won" is a render, not a calculation.
  • The visual language. The filled/hollow strip is already how the settlement card and the in-app hole strip say this. A reuses it rather than inventing a second grammar for the same fact.

The stroke index rides along because it is already there — api_course_holes.handicap is cached for 92.6% of holes, and the client already knows the difference between a real SI and one it estimated (SIEST). The card shows it, and says when it is a guess rather than quietly printing a number as fact.

Scope: live rounds only. Every other screen is designed portrait-first, and unlocking rotation app-wide means auditing the wizard, every sheet and every hero card for a feature nobody asked for there. On iOS that is one view overriding its orientation rather than removing the portrait lock from project.yml.

One thing the card cannot honestly show. Season points are scored per round, not per hole — the bands read a whole round's differential against your index. So the strip marks where the side game paid: match holes, skins, wolf. On a "just score" round there is no strip, because nothing was won hole by hole and drawing one would invent a competition that is not being played.

Mockups are live HTML at 844×390, the app's own tokens and type. They are honest about proportion and density; they are not pixel-for-pixel the shipped components.
