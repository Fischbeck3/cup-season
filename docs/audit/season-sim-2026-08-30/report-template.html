Sixty-four seasons,
played in an afternoon.

You can't run a season start to finish to find out whether the game holds up. So I ran sixty-four of them against the real engine — every structure, both endgames, nine weeks to thirty-nine — and watched the finish from inside the app.

64 seasons 26 configurations 3 replicates each 8,624 counting rounds 576 golfers

Is the play sound?

Yes — for your shape

Solo at 10–12 golfers gave the closest racing of anything tested: 4–5% final margins, five lead changes a season, and zero runaways in nineteen runs. Two-squad leagues went wire-to-wire 70% of the time.

Is the finish exciting?

For two people

The Cup Final reversed the regular season in 5 of 13 solo runs — real drama. But when seeds lock, eight of your ten friends can no longer win, with four weeks to play. And one screen names the wrong champion outright.

Every league was born through the real create_league and lock_league, drawn by the real randomize_squads, and scored by the real close_month, enter_cup_final and close_season. Rounds were inserted exactly the way the client inserts them. Sixty of the sixty-four ran inside transactions that were rolled back; four were committed so these screenshots could be taken.

The shape of a season

The single strongest result in the study is that more contenders means closer racing, and the effect is not subtle. A two-squad league is a coin flip settled in week one; a ten-player individual league goes to the wire every time.

“Wire-to-wire” means the eventual winner led from week one and was never caught. Margin is the winner's lead as a share of their own total.

What a point actually measures

Every round scores in one of five bands. Across 8,624 counting rounds, the distribution is heavily bottom-weighted:

12 · Torched it9.2%
9 · Beat your number10.6%
7 · Played to your index16.2%
6 · A little loose21.2%
5 · Rough day, posted anyway42.8%

Sixty-four percent of all rounds land on 5 or 6. The consequence is arithmetic:

The best golfer in a league out-scores the worst by 1.83 points per round. One extra posted round is worth 6.6 points. Showing up once more is worth about three and a half times being the best player on the course.

Measured inside each league separately — so this is not an artefact of pooling leagues of different sizes — season points correlate +0.96 with rounds counted and +0.04 with how well you played. Rounds-counted was the stronger driver in 61 of 61 leagues. Unanimous.

The cause is the handicap engine doing its job. Across 576 golfers the WHS-lite index settled 4.7 strokes below true playing ability, because it averages your best few differentials. Volatile golfers lose most — improvers and streaky players dropped 6.6–6.8 strokes, steady players 3.5. Since a point comes from playing_index − differential, a lower index makes every band harder to reach. A golfer gets worse at this game by playing it well.

This result is conservative. The simulated golfers reach “played to your index or better” in 35.5% of rounds; real golf is 20–25%. The true distribution would be more bottom-heavy, not less.

The bug at the most important moment

P0 · wrong answer

Home names the wrong champion

The season-wrapped card picks the winner as the top of the points table. The real champion is seasons.champion_member_id, decided by the Cup Final — a different race entirely. In the simulated league Encanto Ten, the database crowned Vic Slice 28–26. Home said “Ray Fairway took it.”

The same block renders “Your name goes on the cup” when you are top of the points table — so the player who did not win the Cup Final can be told that they did. Here, the table was even tied at 99, so the client broke a tie the engine breaks with a documented ladder.

This is precisely the failure the code audit named yesterday — a rule with more than one producer. The champion now has two. The fix is to read the crown from the season row and never infer it from standings.

The eight who can't win

P1 · misleading

Non-finalists are shown a race that is already sealed

When enter_cup_final runs, cup_finalists holds exactly two rows, and close_season crowns only from those two. In a ten-player solo league, eight golfers are mathematically eliminated from the title with four weeks still to play — and Home does not say so.

The number itself is real — he is five points off on the points table, which is still live and still worth the Points King share of the pot. It is the framing that lies: a cup-final header wrapped around points-table numbers.

What already works

Working

The mid-season view for someone who is losing

The golfer in last place is pointed at the person one place ahead — “3 points back of Moe Duffer” — not at the leader, plus a concrete floor nudge. That is the right target to hand someone in tenth, and it is the difference between a league they keep opening and one they don't.

The rest of the findings

P2 · fixed today

The app promised solo leagues a +10 head start that doesn't exist

The endgame explainer stated “The leader carries +10 in” unconditionally. The engine grants it only for two-squad leagues — solo, three-squad and four-squad leaders carry nothing. Your solo pilot would have read a rule the engine does not have, in the copy meant to teach the endgame. I introduced that line yesterday. Fixed and verified in a live solo league; preflight passes.

P2

A finished season still renders as a live race

Opening a completed league shows “SEASON RACE · THE CLIMB” with IN/OUT badges and a “CUT LINE · 15 BACK” — on a season that is settled — while the actual champion sits at #2 with nothing marking him as the winner.

P2

No season can be shorter than three months

season_months is constrained to 3–12, so a four- or six-week trial season cannot be created. Worth knowing a week out from a pilot. Note also that lock_league takes explicit dates, so the stored bylaw and the real window can silently disagree.

P3

The participation floor is structurally inert in solo leagues

close_month walks squad members, and solo leagues have no squads — so no floor penalty, forfeit or auto-bye can ever fire. Confirmed across all 25 completed solo seasons. The copy is honest about it, but Home still renders a progress bar and a “2 MORE · 1D” countdown for something with no consequence.

P3

sandbox_scrap can't scrap a league that closed a month

Fourteen tables reference league_members with ON DELETE NO ACTION. The shipped teardown deletes the league first, on the assumption that the cascade clears those references — it doesn't, once a floor penalty exists. My own teardown hit this and now clears children in dependency order.

What I'd change before the 6th

  1. Fix the champion bug. It fires on the single most important screen in the product, at the one moment a league is guaranteed to be watching.
  2. Run season one on the points table, not the Cup Final. See below.
  3. Aim for ten or twelve golfers, not eight. Eight-player leagues went wire-to-wire in one of three; ten never did.
  4. Decide what a point should mean. If the table should rank golf rather than attendance, the lever is the counting cap — at a cap of two, the bottom bands fell from 64% to 47%, because only your best rounds count. It's one field in the wizard.
  5. Decide whether the solo floor should have teeth — or stop drawing a countdown for something that cannot bite.

The Cup Final is the better story: it overturned months of golf in 38% of runs, which is exactly the drama an endgame is for. But it buys that story by benching eight of your ten friends for the final month of a first pilot — precisely when you most need them still posting. Run season one on the points table, and introduce the Cup Final in season two once the habit exists. If you want the Cup Final anyway, fix the non-finalist card first.
